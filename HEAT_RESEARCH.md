# Heat evidence review — 22 October 2026

This review covers every ID in the 72-row SweetFix workbook. Searches focused on each compound name together with thermal stability, heating, degradation, sweetness retention, and food processing. Primary experiments and official regulator or application data were preferred. The table below records findings that could be connected to a specific ID. A blank new-evidence entry means this search pass did not locate a trustworthy matched time–temperature result; it does **not** prove none exists.

**How to read the evidence types:** `direct` means the named compound or protein was tested. `matrix` means a food, beverage, extract, or commercial preparation was tested; do not transfer the result automatically to a pure compound or different recipe. `dossier` means submitted product data. `qualitative` means an official statement of suitability without a numeric experiment. `regulatory_review` summarizes a regulator's evaluation. `no_new_match` means the previous workbook entry is retained.

The measured outcome also matters. Chemical retention, sweetness retention, protein unfolding, and formation of browning products answer different questions. A melting point or dry-powder thermogram is not a safe oven temperature. Baking decisions still need the ingredient's actual temperature, time, pH, water content, and recipe matrix.

Fifteen IDs still have `NR` in the original workbook and no matched new result in this pass: `SW018`, `SW026`, `SW032`, `SW035`, `SW036`, `SW037`, `SW049`, `SW050`, `SW051`, `SW052`, `SW053`, `SW054`, `SW055`, `SW056`, and `SW063`. These include scarce or uncommon compounds for which search results mostly described identity, sweetness, or extraction rather than a measured food-heating outcome.
